# The Rosling Rules (Presentation Playbook v0.5 draft)

Rules 1–34 are the core playbook (v0.3, live 20 Nov 2026). The skill uses them through its eight steps; their numbers are shown so you can trace each step. Rules 35–49 were adopted 23–24 Nov (v0.4). Rules 50–55 are new in this v0.5 draft for Fidel's review.

## Core (rules 1–34), as the eight steps use them
| Step | Rules | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 North Star and 1NTC | 22, 41 | Say whose North Star it is and who owns the decision, in the first half minute. |
| 2 Question | 4 | Ask one question the viewer will probably get wrong. Hold their guess. |
| 3 Build | 1–3 | Empty stage, then one picture, one part at a time. Name parts in human words. Nothing moves until the viewer can read it. |
| 4 Move | 9–12 | One picture for the whole piece; transform it, never swap slides. Slow down out loud at the hard part. Replay fast. |
| 5 Scene | 17, 30 | A scene, not a fact. Let the viewer feel it before you name it. |
| 6 Straight | 31–34 | Mark claims as research, suspicion or untested. Label every stand-in: generated voice, generated picture, made-up numbers, concept not built. |
| 7 Close | 14, 29 | Clear the stage, say it plainly, end on who does what by when: a name, an action, a date. |
| 8 Explain-back | 35 | One person who wasn't in the meetings watches once and explains it back in their own words. |
| Always | 16 | One shared space: numbers live inside the scene (a receipt, a phone, a hologram in the room). |

The full wording of rules 1–34 is on the v0.3 Rules page. If it isn't in this repo yet, Fidel adds it.

## Adopted 23–24 Nov 2026 (v0.4)
- **35** Explain-back test, sharpened: not a quiz. Whatever they can't say back, the film hasn't said yet.
- **36** Every film page names the size it was made at and links the skill.
- **38** One worked example carries the film; the full rules stay as reference beside it. (Murphy)
- **39** Say whether you are helping someone think or solving their problem. (Murphy)
- **40** Always a movie, photoreal by default. Slides only if asked.
- **41** Name the 1NTC: the one neck to choke, the single person accountable.
- **42** Ask the setup questions in one batch of choices.
- **43** Free by default. Paid services only when named (choosing a paid quality level counts).
- **44** The 1NTC decides; we recommend. "You're the one neck to choke. It's your call."
- **45** Clever questions: one answer covers several things.
- **46** Always offer it when something complex is being explained.
- **47** Claude's own lip-sync beats none.
- **48** Draft first, with the cheapest lip-sync that works.
- **49** The assistant offers it, every time it fits; don't rely on people remembering.

## New in v0.5 (24 Nov 2026), for Fidel's review
- **50 Quality is a menu.** Menu 4 asks: Draft (free, default) · Standard · High · Premium. Nobody gets a flat film by surprise.
- **51 Library first.** Check the shared media library before generating anything. Add what you make afterwards.
- **52 Cast bible.** Recurring people (Freya, Master Jove, Sam, Ruby Red) look the same in every film. Use their reference image when you generate.
- **53 Plain-English scripts.** Write like an aircraft maintenance manual (ASD-STE100 style): short sentences, one idea each, common words, active voice.
- **54 Real people and roles.** Never invent a real person's face. A role (Master Jove) may use a labelled character-concept face. Get pronouns right.
- **55 Show the menus.** When explaining the skill itself, show pictures of the menus and how to answer them.
